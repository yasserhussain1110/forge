## Summary

### Chapter 1

Chapter 1 talks about vectors. Vectors are objects with magnitude and direction.

For example - An arrow pointing top right, is a vector. An arrow pointing to bottom right is a vector. Arrows pointing to the left or right are vectors.

Vectors can also be added together using the triangle rule.

### Chapter 2

Chapter 2 talks about linear combinations, span and basis vectors.

Linear combination is scaling two vectors and summing them up. So if you have x vector and y vector, you could create a * x + y * b vector.

Span is all vectors which can be reached via any linear combination of two vectors.

Basis vectors are the smallest set of vectors whose linear combination creates the entire span space.

### Chapter 3

Chapter 3 talks about linear transformation and matrices.

Linear transformation is moving a vector to a different location. If you are linearly transforming the basis vectors then you are transforming the entire span space.

Matrices can be thought of linear transformations. For example - 

[[a c]
 [b d]]

This matrix could be thought of applying linear transformation to base vectors i cap and j cap.
After this transformation the i cap would end up being - ai + bj and j cap would end up being ci + dj.


### Chapter 4

Chapter 4 then talks of matrix multiplication and how it could be thought of as applying a series of linear transformation on a particular vector.
